Why Your SME Needs a Multilingual Website to Go Global

A short guide for small business owners

An overseas buyer who finds your product on social media will usually do one more thing before ordering: search for your business. What they find, or fail to find, shapes whether they trust you enough to reach out.

What a bilingual website does for you

  • Builds trust. A clear site in the buyer's language tells them you are a real, organized business.
  • Gets you found. Pages written in English can appear in searches from people who never type in Indonesian.
  • Answers questions while you sleep. Products, prices, and how to order are visible at any hour, across time zones.
  • Makes ordering easy. A pre-filled WhatsApp or email message removes friction for first-time buyers.

Do it properly, not just translated

  • Separate pages per language. Give each language its own address so search engines can index both correctly.
  • Write for the reader. Adapt wording to how overseas buyers think about your product instead of translating word for word.
  • Show prices in a familiar currency. Seeing USD removes a mental conversion step.
  • State your hours and time zone. It sets clear expectations on when you reply.
  • Make it fast on mobile. Many visitors will open your site on a phone with a modest connection.

Start small

You do not need a large website. A focused landing page or a short company profile in two languages already gives you a credible home on the web, and it can grow as your business does.
